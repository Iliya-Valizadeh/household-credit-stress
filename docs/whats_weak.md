# What's weak

The full list of limits. The README shows the top few. Rank them by how much each one
could change the main result, with the largest first. Say what was done about each
one, even if the answer is "nothing yet".

| Rank | Weakness | How much it could change the result | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The `arx` model behind the live one- and three-month ledger forecasts does not beat the AR(1) [baseline](glossary.md#baseline). The test [MAE](glossary.md#mean-absolute-error) gap is 0.03 bp at one month and 0.17 bp at three months, and both [confidence intervals](glossary.md#confidence-interval) include zero (`CLAIMS.md`) | This is the model the ledger grades every month. If it adds nothing over the simplest rule, the ledger's point forecasts carry no proven edge | Kept `arx` and reported the result as it is, rather than swapping in AR(1) to make the headline look better; the verdict field in `reports/metrics.json` stays visible next to every number it is next to |
| 2 | The scenario engine's `pooled_arx` model (ADR 0004) also fails its own pre-registered twelve- and twenty-four-month check against AR(1): the gap is 1.25 bp at twelve months and 1.64 bp at twenty-four months, and both intervals include zero (`CLAIMS.md`) | This is the model behind every user-drawn scenario path. The result means the stress test's two-year response is not shown as evidence that the drivers forecast anything | Labelled every scenario "Illustrative: not a forecast", per the rule the eval plan fixed before this result was seen (docs/eval_plan.md, "Changes to this plan") |
| 3 | Fitted region by region, the `arx` driver slopes are unstable: at the last fitted origin the mortgage rate slope comes out negative for ATL and MB, the opposite of the expected sign (`reports/metrics.json#model.coefficients_last_origin`) | A per-region model could show a rate rise lowering arrears in those two regions, which no one should believe | The scenario engine pools the slopes across all seven regions instead of fitting one set per region, and drops a wrong-signed driver at each origin (ADR 0004, "the sign rule") |
| 4 | History from before this repo's own `data/raw/cba/` archive started exists only as the latest published [vintage](glossary.md#vintage); revisions the CBA, Statistics Canada or the Bank of Canada made to old months are invisible (docs/eval_plan.md) | [Backtest](glossary.md#backtest) numbers over that span could look better or worse than a forecaster working at the time really would have done, because the backtest unintentionally sees the final, revised values | Stated as a standing caveat next to every backtest number in docs/eval_plan.md and MODEL_CARD.md; not measured, since no earlier vintage is on hand to compare against |
| 5 | The scenario fit drops every row touching March 2020 to December 2021, when payment deferrals kept arrears down while unemployment rose (ADR 0004, "Context") | A future downturn that comes with a deferral program would not look like any scenario this tool can draw | Dropped those rows from the fit, and said in ADR 0004 that every scenario assumes no deferral program |
| 6 | The web page's scenario range check (`site/app.js`) only looks at each driver's level, not the `x_u`/`x_m` term the Python engine also checks (`scenario.driver_ranges`, ADR 0004) | Could let the page's "beyond history" warning or hard stop fire at a different point than the Python engine would, in either direction | The page clamps its sliders and warns using the level range only, exported in `site/data/scenario.json`; it never computes or checks the term range |
